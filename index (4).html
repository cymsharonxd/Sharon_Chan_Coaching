When I first met Sharon, I was going through one of the most challenging periods of my life. For most of my life, I had tied my self-worth to achievement, working hard in high school to get into a top college, pushing myself in college to land a prestigious job, and then spending the next four years working 100-hour weeks in finance. When I started facing setbacks at work and felt like my efforts weren't being recognized, I completely lost my sense of direction. I felt burned out, anxious, and like very few people truly understood what I was going through.

I met Sharon through a mutual friend. At first, we were just casually talking about life, but what immediately stood out to me was how observant and perceptive she was. She listened without judgment, asked thoughtful questions, and somehow understood what I was trying to say even when I couldn't fully articulate it myself. For the first time in a long time, I genuinely felt heard.

I wanted to make a change in my life, but I had no idea where to begin, so I decided to give life coaching with Sharon a try. What I appreciated most was that her coaching didn't stop at helping me process my emotions. It combined the emotional support I would expect from therapy with concrete next steps that I could actually implement. I struggle with anxiety, and in the past, I often left therapy feeling lighter emotionally but unsure how to create real change in my daily life. Sharon always made sure I walked away with practical actions, frameworks, or habits to work on. Having tangible progress to make each week gave me something to look forward to and slowly helped me regain a sense of control over my life.

One example that really stuck with me was how overwhelmed I felt by the constant stream of ideas in my head. It felt like everything was happening at once, and I didn't know where to start. Sharon encouraged me to simply write everything down on paper without worrying about organization, and to work through it one step at a time. As I mapped out my thoughts, priorities naturally became clearer. What had previously felt like complete chaos became manageable.

She also helped me with something that seemed small but had a surprisingly big impact. Because of my anxiety (and a bit of OCD), I had developed a habit of obsessively clearing my inbox before starting any meaningful work, even if I had important projects to complete. I couldn't focus until every email was organized. Instead of dismissing it as a minor issue, Sharon took it seriously and helped me build a simple system around it: a recurring 15-minute "email reset" every Sunday evening. Rather than carrying the mental burden of constantly reminding myself to clean my inbox, I could trust that I'd have dedicated time each week to do it. That one small habit significantly reduced my anxiety and made Mondays feel much more manageable.

What I appreciate most about Sharon is that she doesn't just listen; she helps you translate overwhelming thoughts into practical, actionable steps. She has a rare combination of empathy and structure. I feel like her background in product management contributes to how naturally she breaks down complicated problems into manageable pieces, but she does it in a way that feels incredibly supportive rather than prescriptive.

I would especially recommend Sharon to people who are feeling stuck during a career transition, struggling with burnout or anxiety, overwhelmed by too many competing thoughts, or simply looking for more direction in life. If you're someone who doesn't just want to talk about your challenges but wants someone who will help you build a realistic plan to move forward, I genuinely think you'll get a lot out of working with her.

The person behind the practice

About

Sharon was born and raised in Hong Kong. At 18, she moved to the U.S. alone for college, with no family nearby, building a new life from scratch at the University of Pennsylvania, where she studied Communications and Consumer Psychology. After graduating, she stayed in New York City on her own, chasing the life she'd pictured for herself: a career in tech and finance in one of the most competitive cities in the world.

That path came with real struggle. Self-doubt, burnout, and constantly second-guessing her own choices, often without anyone close by to talk it through with. Her career took her through marketing and PR internships, sales at Salesforce, and eventually product management at Mastercard, where she grew steadily over several years across regional and global teams.

The turning point came when a friend told her, almost in passing, that she'd make a good coach. That comment led Sharon to work with a coach herself, and for the first time, she experienced firsthand what coaching actually does. It reframed something for her: the most powerful thing isn't being told what to do or what to think. It's arriving at your own answers. Her coach never made a single decision for her. She asked the right questions and let Sharon find her own way through, and that experience is what led Sharon to the Co-Active Training Institute, where she completed 104 hours of training to become a certified Co-Active coach herself.

For Sharon, coaching isn't about having all the answers. It's a partnership: one that helps clients find clarity and design the life they actually want, the same way it was done for her.

How we'll work together

Approach
Grounded

We start with where you actually are

Not the version of your life you think you should present. Sessions start with an honest read of the present: what's working, what's costing you, what you've been avoiding saying out loud.

Directional

Clarity before action

Most people don't need more options. They need to know which direction is actually theirs, so we narrow before we plan, so the steps you take are steps you'll keep taking.

Accountable

Built to hold you to it

Insight fades without follow-through. Between sessions you'll have concrete commitments, not vague intentions, and space to be honest about what actually happened.
